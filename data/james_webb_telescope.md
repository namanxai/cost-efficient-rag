# The James Webb Space Telescope

The James Webb Space Telescope, often abbreviated JWST, launched on December 25, 2021, aboard an Ariane 5 rocket from French Guiana. It is a joint mission led by NASA in partnership with the European Space Agency and the Canadian Space Agency, designed as the scientific successor to the Hubble Space Telescope.

Unlike Hubble, which primarily observes in visible and ultraviolet light, JWST is optimized for infrared astronomy, allowing it to peer through cosmic dust clouds and observe some of the earliest galaxies formed after the Big Bang. Its primary mirror is 6.5 meters in diameter, made of 18 hexagonal gold-coated beryllium segments, giving it far greater light-collecting area than Hubble's 2.4 meter mirror.

JWST orbits the Sun at the second Lagrange point, known as L2, located about 1.5 million kilometers from Earth, where it stays in a stable position relative to the Earth and Sun while a five-layer sunshield keeps its instruments extremely cold. Since beginning science operations in July 2022, the telescope has captured detailed images of the Carina Nebula, examined the atmospheres of exoplanets for potential biosignatures, and detected some of the most distant galaxies ever observed, dating to within a few hundred million years of the Big Bang.
