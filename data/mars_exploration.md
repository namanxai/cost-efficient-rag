# Mars Exploration

Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun and is often called the Red Planet because of iron oxide on its surface. NASA's Perseverance rover landed in Jezero Crater on February 18, 2021, tasked with searching for signs of ancient microbial life and collecting rock samples for a future return to Earth. Perseverance carries a small helicopter named Ingenuity, which became the first aircraft to achieve powered, controlled flight on another planet, completing its first flight on April 19, 2021.

The Curiosity rover, which landed in Gale Crater in August 2012, discovered evidence that the region once held a lake with water that could have supported microbial life. Curiosity uses a rock-vaporizing laser called ChemCam to analyze the chemical composition of Martian rocks from a distance.

Mars has two small moons, Phobos and Deimos, which are thought to be captured asteroids. A day on Mars, called a sol, lasts about 24 hours and 39 minutes, only slightly longer than a day on Earth. However, a Martian year lasts about 687 Earth days because Mars is farther from the Sun and takes longer to complete an orbit.

Average surface temperatures on Mars are around -63 degrees Celsius, and the atmosphere is about 95 percent carbon dioxide, far too thin to support liquid water on the surface today, although evidence suggests rivers and lakes existed billions of years ago. Future crewed missions to Mars, discussed by NASA's Artemis program planners, would likely take six to nine months to reach the planet using current propulsion technology.
