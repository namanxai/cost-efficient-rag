# The International Space Station and Low Earth Orbit

The International Space Station, or ISS, is a joint project between NASA, Roscosmos, JAXA, ESA, and the Canadian Space Agency. It has been continuously occupied since November 2, 2000, making it one of the longest continuous human presences in space. The station orbits Earth at an altitude of roughly 400 kilometers and completes an orbit approximately every 90 minutes, meaning astronauts on board see about 16 sunrises and sunsets each day.

The ISS serves as a microgravity laboratory where astronauts conduct experiments in biology, physics, astronomy, and materials science that would be difficult or impossible to perform on Earth. Crews typically rotate every six months, though some astronauts, such as Frank Rubio, have stayed for over a year, providing valuable data on the effects of long-duration spaceflight on the human body, including bone density loss and muscle atrophy.

Cargo resupply missions to the ISS are flown by commercial companies including SpaceX, which uses its Dragon spacecraft, and Northrop Grumman, which uses the Cygnus spacecraft. SpaceX's Crew Dragon capsule has also carried astronauts to and from the station since its first crewed flight in 2020, ending American reliance on Russian Soyuz rockets for crew transport for a period of time.

NASA and its partners have announced plans to deorbit the ISS around 2031, after which low Earth orbit activity is expected to shift toward commercial space stations currently in development by companies such as Axiom Space and Voyager Space.
